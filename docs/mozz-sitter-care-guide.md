# Mozz’s sitter care guide

**Status: Owner-approved.**
Prepared from the owner interview. “Confirmed” means explicitly reported by the owner, not independently verified by a veterinarian. Unanswered questions and treatment-name uncertainty are listed separately.

## Confirmed care instructions

### About Mozz
- Mozz is an adult female dog.
- She will stay at the sitter’s home.
- She has separation anxiety and takes approximately three days to feel at home somewhere new. This is not a known time for settling during an individual crate session.

### Feeding
- Feed twice daily: once in the morning and once at night.
- **At each meal:** ½ cup Purina dry kibble mixed with ¼ package wet food.
- The owner says the wet food must be refrigerated. The dry food is kibble.
- No other food is part of her reported diet.
- The wet food may be **A Pup Above, chicken mix**; the owner was unsure and intended to check. Do not treat the brand or recipe as confirmed.

### Bathroom breaks and exercise
- Let Mozz into the backyard as frequently as possible, with morning and evening bathroom breaks as the minimum.
- The owner considers backyard activity sufficient exercise.
- Avoid walks as much as possible: she dislikes streets and cars.
- If a walk is necessary, expect strong pulling toward home or wherever she considers safe.
- Whenever outdoors, she must be very securely contained.
- The owner permits her to be alone in a securely enclosed backyard. Time and weather limits remain unspecified.
- The owner reports that the house is her safe place and does not expect her to try to escape from it. This is not a guarantee against escape.

### Sleep, crate, and time alone
- At the sitter’s home, she sleeps in her crate with her bed, with a sheet covering the crate.
- At home, she normally sleeps in bed with her parents; that is background information, not the sitter’s sleeping arrangement.
- Use the covered crate whenever she is left alone. The owner reports she will pee on the floor if left loose alone.
- **Maximum time alone in her crate: four hours.**
- She may initially be worried, then settle and rest. Outside noises may cause her to bark.
- The usual time to settle during a crate session is unknown. Additional calming instructions and an escalation threshold are also unknown.

### People, handling, and other animals
- Do not pick her up: she dislikes it and tries to get away.
- Put her in her crate before unfamiliar people enter.
- She may growl and rush toward unfamiliar visitors; the owner describes this as looking aggressive and potentially scary.
- The owner reports she has never bitten anyone. The expectation that she will only sniff a visitor is not a guarantee of safe contact.
- The owner reports she is fine with any other dogs. Specific introduction, feeding, and toy-sharing rules were not provided.
- Behavior around children and non-dog animals remains unknown.

### Paw licking and allergies
- Mozz has allergies and may lick her paws until they bleed.
- Interrupt licking by covering her paws with a blanket, distracting her, or verbally calling her attention to it. The verbal interruption may work only temporarily.
- If her paws bleed, the owner wants veterinary care.
- Contact her parents first before arranging the allergy treatment, except that the serious-emergency authorization below applies when immediate emergency care is needed.
- **Regular vet: Whipple Avenue Pet Hospital.**
- The injection’s exact identity needs veterinary confirmation. No injection dose or administration instruction is established by this guide.

### Monthly medication
- Due on the **4th of every month**.
- The owner’s supplied product link identifies **Simparica Trio**, and the owner will leave it for the sitter.
- The linked product is described as the 22.1–44 lb presentation. This does not establish Mozz’s weight, prescription, or dose.
- Exact strength, amount, administration directions, last dose, and the method for preventing a duplicate dose remain unconfirmed. This guide is not a complete dosing instruction.

### Contact and emergencies
- Routine concerns: **text first, then call**.
- Emergencies: **call immediately**.
- For a serious emergency, if neither parent can be reached: **seek emergency veterinary care immediately and keep trying to contact them**.
- The owner intends to add backup emergency contacts; none have been supplied yet.
- An emergency spending limit was not supplied.
